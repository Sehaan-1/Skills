# Board: Invoices screen, 2026-09-17

Tiles cast before the arms were written. Same screen, same real copy, three directions. Ratio 16:10.

| Tile | Register | File | One line |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Keep | `docs/heraldry/boards/invoices-a.png` | Paper, hairlines, a table you can trust with your eyes half-closed |
| B | Scriptorium | `docs/heraldry/boards/invoices-b.png` | Wider margins, softer paper, an editorial tone that flatters the empty state |
| C | Armoury (outlier) | `docs/heraldry/boards/invoices-c.png` | Dark, monospaced, a ledger a terminal user would like |

## Chosen

Tile A, in the owner's words: *"the other two look like someone else's product. I want the one that looks like my paperwork."*

Rejected:

- **Tile B** looked calm but read slow at 24 rows; the serif column headings made the table feel like a magazine and the money was harder to scan.
- **Tile C** was the nicest picture and the wrong life: this is used at a kitchen table in daylight, next to a paper folder, not in a terminal.

## What the tiles taught us

- The warm neutral is not a preference, it is the brand (it matches the printed invoices), so the arms keep `#F7F6F3` and build everything else around it.
- Money wants monospace and right alignment; both alternatives showed how badly proportional digits scan in a column.
- The outlier earned its place by ruling out the whole dark direction in one look, which no amount of describing would have settled.

## Prompts used

> A desktop interface for Saltbox, a small-business bookkeeping app, on the Invoices screen. Register: Keep, solid and unsurprising. Colors: Paper #F7F6F3 for the page, Surface #FFFFFF for raised fills, Ink #1B1A18 for text, Slate #6B6A66 for secondary text, Copper #A2542E for one accent. Type: Geist, with tabular numerals for every amount. Density: high, tables separated by hairlines rather than cards. The frame implies nothing moves except a row hover. Banned: gradients, glow, a second accent, cards around table data. Ratio 16:10.

The other two prompts used the same scene with the register, palette, and type stance swapped from the register table, and the register's never-list moved into the banned clause.
